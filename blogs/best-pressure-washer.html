Quick Buying Tip

Never buy a pressure washer based only on PSI. Water flow (LPM), pump quality, motor power, and intended application are equally important.

What is a Pressure Washer?

A pressure washer is a powerful cleaning machine that sprays water at high pressure to remove dirt, mud, grease, algae, paint, oil, cement residue, and other contaminants from various surfaces.

Unlike a normal garden hose, a pressure washer combines high pressure with controlled water flow, making cleaning significantly faster while reducing water consumption.

Important Specifications Explained

Pressure determines the cleaning force.

  • 90–120 Bar — Home cleaning
  • 130–170 Bar — Cars & bikes
  • 180–220 Bar — Workshops
  • 250+ Bar — Industrial cleaning

Many buyers ignore water flow.

Higher pressure without enough water cannot clean efficiently.

A higher LPM allows dirt to be flushed away faster.

  • 5–7 LPM → Home Use
  • 8–10 LPM → Commercial
  • 11–18 LPM → Heavy Duty

Motor power determines continuous performance.

Power Recommended Use
1200–1500W Light Home Cleaning
1800–2200W Cars & Workshops
2500W+ Commercial & Industrial

Brass Pump vs Aluminium Pump

Brass Pump

  • Long lifespan
  • Better heat resistance
  • Suitable for daily usage
  • Industrial applications
  • Higher efficiency

Aluminium Pump

  • Lower price
  • Lightweight
  • Ideal for occasional home use
  • Easy maintenance
  • Less durable

Electric vs Petrol Pressure Washers

One of the biggest questions buyers ask is whether they should choose an electric pressure washer or a petrol-powered pressure washer. The answer depends entirely on where and how frequently you intend to use the machine.

Pressure Washer Accessories You Should Buy

Foam Cannon

Produces thick foam for safe car washing while reducing swirl marks and improving cleaning quality.

Turbo Nozzle

Creates a rotating high-pressure spray for removing stubborn dirt, moss, algae, grease and concrete residue.

Extension Lance

Perfect for cleaning rooftops, trucks, walls and hard-to-reach areas safely.

Common Buying Mistakes

Avoid these expensive mistakes before buying:

  • Buying based only on PSI.
  • Ignoring water flow (LPM).
  • Choosing plastic pumps for heavy-duty work.
  • Using undersized extension cables.
  • Buying without checking spare parts availability.
  • Using dirty water without a filter.
  • Ignoring motor duty cycle.
  • Choosing the wrong nozzle.
  • Purchasing without warranty support.
  • Not considering future workload.

Pressure Washer Maintenance Tips

  • Release pressure completely.
  • Drain remaining water.
  • Clean inlet filter.
  • Inspect hose for leaks.

  • Lubricate moving components.
  • Inspect seals and O-rings.
  • Tighten loose fittings.
  • Clean spray gun and nozzles.

Pressure Washer Safety Tips

High-pressure water can cause serious injury if used incorrectly.

  • Always wear safety goggles.
  • Use waterproof footwear.
  • Never point the spray at people.
  • Disconnect power before servicing.
  • Keep children away.
  • Use clean water only.
  • Read the user manual.
  • Use proper extension cords.
  • Check electrical grounding.
  • Store indoors after use.

Final Buying Recommendation

For most homeowners, a 120–150 Bar electric pressure washer provides excellent value for cleaning cars, bikes, patios and home exteriors.

Professional workshops should consider 180–220 Bar machines with brass pumps, while industries, farms and construction sites benefit from 250+ Bar heavy-duty pressure washers capable of continuous operation.

Conclusion

Choosing the right pressure washer isn't just about selecting the highest pressure rating. Consider your cleaning requirements, water flow, motor power, pump quality, accessories, warranty, and spare parts availability. A properly selected pressure washer saves time, reduces water consumption, extends equipment life, and delivers professional-quality cleaning for years.
