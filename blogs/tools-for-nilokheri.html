Choosing the Right Power Tool

Buying the cheapest machine isn't always the best decision. Selecting equipment based on your daily workload helps improve productivity while reducing maintenance costs.

Before purchasing, consider:

  • Material being cut or drilled
  • Required motor power
  • Availability of accessories
  • Warranty and service support
  • Ease of spare parts availability
  • Operator safety features
  • Weight and portability

Essential Safety Tips

Always follow these safety practices while operating power tools:

  • Wear approved safety goggles.
  • Use hearing protection during extended work.
  • Wear suitable gloves whenever appropriate.
  • Inspect power cords before use.
  • Use the correct cutting or grinding accessory.
  • Never remove factory-installed safety guards.
  • Disconnect power before changing accessories.
  • Keep the work area clean and well illuminated.
  • Avoid loose clothing near rotating equipment.
  • Read the manufacturer's operating instructions.

Maintenance Tips to Increase Tool Life

Keep Tools Clean

Remove dust and debris after every use to improve cooling and reduce wear.

Inspect Carbon Brushes

Replace worn brushes before they damage the motor or armature.

Lubricate Moving Parts

Follow the manufacturer's maintenance schedule for gears and bearings.

Store Properly

Keep tools in a dry, dust-free environment to prevent corrosion and electrical damage.
