Drill Bit Selection Guide: Choose the Right Drill Bit for Every Material

Using the correct drill bit is just as important as choosing the right drill. The wrong bit produces rough holes, wears out quickly and may even damage your machine. This guide explains different drill bit types, their applications and how to select the best one for every job.

Why Drill Bit Selection Matters

Every material requires a different cutting geometry, hardness and coating. A drill bit designed for wood cannot efficiently drill hardened steel, while a masonry bit performs poorly on aluminium.

Using the wrong drill bit can lead to:
  • Broken drill bits
  • Poor hole quality
  • Overheating
  • Reduced drilling speed
  • Motor overload
  • Unsafe operation

Which Drill Bit Should You Use?

Wood

Brad Point, Auger and Hole Saw bits provide clean, accurate holes without damaging the wood fibres.

Metal

HSS, Cobalt and Titanium-coated bits are suitable for mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium.

Concrete

Use Masonry bits with Impact Drills or SDS Plus bits with Rotary Hammers for best performance.

Choosing the Correct Size

Select the drill bit diameter according to the screw, anchor or fastener you intend to install. Oversized holes reduce holding strength, while undersized holes make installation difficult.

Tip: Always mark the drilling depth using masking tape or a drill stop to avoid drilling deeper than necessary.

Tips for Longer Drill Bit Life

✔ Use the correct drilling speed.
✔ Apply steady pressure instead of excessive force.
✔ Use cutting oil while drilling metal.
✔ Keep drill bits clean after use.
✔ Store bits in a dedicated drill bit case.
✔ Replace dull bits before they overheat.

Buying Checklist

Before purchasing drill bits, check for:

  • Correct material type
  • Suitable diameter
  • Machine compatibility
  • Quality coating
  • Trusted manufacturer
  • Available replacement sizes
  • Corrosion resistance
  • Storage case included
