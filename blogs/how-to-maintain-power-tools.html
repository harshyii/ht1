Why Regular Maintenance Matters

Every power tool operates under heavy mechanical load. Dust, heat, vibration and moisture slowly reduce performance over time. Without routine maintenance, motors work harder, bearings wear faster and electrical components become unreliable.

A few minutes of maintenance after every job can significantly increase the lifespan of your equipment while reducing downtime and repair expenses.

Professional Tip

Most power tool failures occur because of poor maintenance rather than manufacturing defects.

Daily Maintenance Checklist

✔ Remove dust and debris from the tool body.
✔ Clean ventilation openings.
✔ Inspect the power cable or battery.
✔ Check switches for smooth operation.
✔ Wipe the tool using a dry microfiber cloth.
✔ Store the tool in a dry, dust-free location.

Maintenance Schedule

Interval Maintenance Task
After Every Use Clean the tool, remove dust and inspect accessories.
Weekly Inspect switches, power cable, safety guards and fasteners.
Monthly Check bearings, chuck, ventilation and carbon brushes.
Every 6 Months Lubricate gearbox (if applicable).
Annually Professional servicing and complete inspection.

Common Maintenance Mistakes

Avoid These
  • Ignoring unusual vibration
  • Blocking ventilation holes
  • Using damaged cutting discs
  • Operating with worn brushes
  • Using the wrong accessories
  • Storing tools while dirty
Best Practices
  • Clean after every use
  • Store in protective cases
  • Replace worn accessories early
  • Use genuine spare parts
  • Inspect before every job
  • Follow manufacturer instructions

Signs Your Tool Needs Service

  • Burning smell during operation
  • Unusual vibration
  • Motor overheating
  • Excessive sparks
  • Broken housing
  • Damaged power cable
  • Reduced cutting performance
